# Purpose-driven literature reading

Read literature to answer a current question—not to fill a generic review template or manufacture a research gap. Ask what the user wants from the paper if the purpose is genuinely unclear and would change the analysis; otherwise infer it from context.

## Choose an appropriate lens

- **Diagnostic reading:** What phenomenon did the authors encounter? What mechanism did they propose, and what observations, tests, or controls were used to locate it? Does that provide a concrete next check for us?
- **Solution reading:** What behavior needed changing? What mechanism does the method exploit? Why does this architecture/intervention fit that mechanism? What did each decisive experiment show?
- **Research-position reading:** What has already been tested and under which conditions? What is known, unresolved, or contested? Does our question or constraint differ materially? No claim of novelty without a meaningful search.
- **Research-thinking reading:** How did the authors abstract and simplify messy reality into a tractable question? What assumptions, omissions, choice of representations, diagrams, and experimental designs made that possible? What can transfer as a way of thinking?

These are purposes, not separate agents or mandatory phases.

## Two reading scales

**Macro — the research story**

- What real or scientific problem is being addressed, and why is it worth asking?
- What abstraction or simplification turns reality into a researchable system? What does it intentionally leave out?
- What are the core hypotheses, conceptual contribution, key figures, and line of argument?
- How does the chosen problem frame privilege some answers while excluding others?

**Micro — decisions and evidence**

- At a key difficulty, why was this method chosen and how does it work?
- What observable predictions follow from the mechanism claim?
- Which comparisons, ablations, slices, or other evidence demonstrate **effectiveness**? Which, if any, establish the **claimed mechanism**?
- What conditions limit interpretation and transfer?

Read the macro or micro scale only as deeply as required. Inspect important figures, method details, appendices, or implementations when they materially affect the answer; do not summarize everything by default.

## Transfer to current research

Conclude with only the useful transfer, for example:

- a new diagnostic hypothesis or concrete test;
- a reusable way to abstract a real-world problem;
- a relevant intervention mechanism;
- a known limitation or an alternative problem formulation;
- a better interpretation of existing evidence.

Be explicit about **paper claims**, **observed results**, and **our extrapolation** when they are materially different. The polished narrative in a paper is not necessarily the author's true chronological discovery path. Do not treat a missing keyword match as proof that no prior work exists. Cite or link verifiable sources when available; say when access is incomplete.
